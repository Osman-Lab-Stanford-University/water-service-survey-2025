# Household Water Service Survey (2025)

**English and Tamil Research Reference Questionnaires**

Osman Lab | Stanford University

## 1. Overview

The Household Water Service Survey was developed by the Osman Lab at Stanford University to examine household experiences of water-service delivery across heterogeneous water provision arrangements.

The instrument captures multiple dimensions of household water service, including affordability, accessibility, reliability, supply characteristics, acceptability, perceived water quality, quantity and provider responsiveness. It also collects household and socioeconomic characteristics, water-related experiences and respondents' assessments of the importance of different service dimensions.

The survey was implemented in September 2025, primarily in Tamil Nadu, South India, with field activities in Karur, Salem and Namakkal.

This repository provides English and Tamil research reference questionnaires and supporting documentation to facilitate scientific interpretation, reproducibility and potential adaptation in other research settings.

## 3. Study design and administration

Fieldwork was conducted in September 2025, primarily in Tamil Nadu, South India, with field activities in Karur, Salem and Namakkal. The study used a nonprobability convenience sampling approach coordinated by the study investigator, with assistance from local partners for field support and survey logistics.

Survey access information, including the questionnaire link and password, was shared through local networks, and the study investigator coordinated field activities.

Eligible respondents were adults aged 18 years or older serving as informants about their households' water-service experiences. Most respondents were college students, although college enrollment was not an eligibility requirement. Many completed the questionnaire during class time, but participation was not restricted to classrooms or organized sessions. Respondents could also participate independently, and individual questionnaire completion locations were not systematically recorded.

Participants completed the questionnaire independently through password-protected Qualtrics after reviewing electronic consent information. The questionnaire was available in English and Tamil. Participation was voluntary, and respondents could discontinue at any time. Local partners provided field support and survey logistics rather than obtaining informed consent, collecting or entering questionnaire responses or accessing identifiable survey data.

Survey access information was initially distributed through investigator-coordinated activities. Incidental onward sharing through recipients' personal networks may have occurred, but this was neither directed nor systematically monitored by the research team. The study did not implement a planned chain-referral sampling procedure. All submissions were subject to the same eligibility and quality-assurance screening procedures irrespective of recruitment channel.

Because the study used nonprobability convenience sampling, the resulting observations should not be interpreted as population-representative estimates.

## 4. Research ethics and participant data protection

The study was conducted under **Stanford University Institutional Review Board (IRB) Protocol No. 82168**, with **Khalid K. Osman serving as Protocol Director**.

Participants received study information and provided electronic informed consent before participating. Participation was voluntary, and respondents could skip questions or discontinue participation.

The study was administered as a confidential research survey. Names and email addresses were requested voluntarily for study-related administrative purposes, including participation certificates or raffle administration. These fields were not used to construct the WSQS or as predictors in the reported models.

For analytical use, the study distinguished survey responses from directly identifying administrative information. The public research archive does not include individual household responses or participant-level records.

The reference questionnaires retain blank administrative questions that formed part of the survey, but contain no completed respondent information, names or email addresses.

The original electronic consent text is not reproduced in these public reference editions and remains part of the restricted study documentation.

## 5. Questionnaire development and bilingual review

The questionnaire was developed to capture household-reported service conditions, household characteristics and priorities across different water provision systems.

The instrument was developed in English and translated into Tamil by the study investigator. Five independent Tamil-speaking reviewers assessed translation accuracy and clarity, with additional feedback from local partners.

The review process emphasized preserving the intended meaning, conceptual structure, response categories and response ordering rather than literal word-for-word correspondence. Minor differences in phrasing reflect natural language usage and local context.

The English and Tamil versions share the same numbered question structure and intended response ordering. The translation process should not be interpreted as establishing formal cross-language measurement invariance.

## 6. Questionnaire structure

The instrument contains **106 numbered questions across 15 thematic sections**.

| Section | Subject |
|---|---|
| S1 | Water sources and use |
| S2 | Cost and affordability |
| S3 | Accessibility |
| S4 | Reliability |
| S5 | Supply characteristics |
| S6 | Acceptability |
| S7 | Perceived water quality |
| S8 | Water quantity |
| S9 | Customer service and maintenance |
| S10 | Water education and awareness |
| S11 | Gender-related aspects |
| S12 | Health |
| S13 | Supply inconsistencies |
| S14 | Resilience |
| S15 | Household and respondent demographics |

An additional administrative section contains contact and participation-certificate questions.

Question formats include single-response choices, multiple-response selections, numerical entries, percentage allocations and 0–10 importance ratings.

The questionnaire distinguishes primary domestic water provision from primary drinking-water provision. It also covers aspects of household experience extending beyond the eight service-quality domains subsequently used in the Water Service Quality Score (WSQS).

**Interpretation of Q6.1**

Q6.1 measures respondents' satisfaction with their current primary water supply source. It should not be interpreted as an independent measure of overall water-service satisfaction.

Individual studies use different subsets of the questionnaire according to their research objectives and analytical designs.

## 7. Data quality and analytical use

The original study applied data-quality screening before constructing analytical datasets.

Screening considered survey completeness, response validity, response-timing information and indicators of potentially invalid or automated submissions. Observations that did not meet the study's quality requirements were excluded from the corresponding analytical sample.

Subsequent research applications implemented additional eligibility, variable-completeness and analytical restrictions appropriate to their respective research questions.

Detailed sample selection, exclusion criteria, measurement construction and analysis procedures are documented in the associated research manuscripts and reproducibility materials.

This repository documents the original survey instrument and its reference presentation. It does not contain household-level responses, raw Qualtrics exports or executable study-specific analytical pipelines.

## 8. Research applications

The questionnaire supports several related investigations of household water-service delivery.

**Water Service Quality Score and predictive modeling**

The npj Clean Water manuscript, *Operational Service Conditions are the primary predictors of Household Water Service Quality Across Heterogeneous Provision Systems*, develops and evaluates the WSQS and investigates the predictive information contained in reported service conditions, household context and stated priorities.

**Standardized comparisons across provision arrangements**

The Water Resources Research manuscript, *A Framework for Standardizing Household Water Service Quality Comparisons Across Primary Drinking-Water Provision Arrangements*, develops a population-standardization framework for comparing household water-service quality across different primary water provision arrangements while accounting for measured household characteristics.

**Additional research applications**

The questionnaire also supports research on household water-service priorities and the value of individualized information in infrastructure service decisions.

**Related measurement and analysis toolkit**

The separate [WSQS toolkit](https://github.com/Osman-Lab-Stanford-University/wsqs-toolkit) provides scoring specifications, measurement guidance and analytical resources for applying the Water Service Quality Score.

This repository documents the underlying survey instrument and is independently versionable. Research articles and analysis repositories should be cited separately when their specific methods or results are used.

## 9. Research reference editions

The English and Tamil documents are professionally formatted research reference editions prepared from the 2025 survey materials.

The reference editions preserve the substantive questionnaire structure, question identifiers and intended response ordering. Historical consent text is excluded, while blank administrative questions are retained.

Minor editorial presentation adjustments and source-to-reference code mappings are documented in [`documentation/PROVENANCE.md`](documentation/PROVENANCE.md) and [`documentation/response_code_crosswalk.csv`](documentation/response_code_crosswalk.csv).

The reference editions are not substitutes for the original programmed Qualtrics instrument. Preparation of these documents does not modify historical survey responses, analytical datasets or previously reported results.
